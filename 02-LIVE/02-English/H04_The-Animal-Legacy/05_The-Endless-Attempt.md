
# The Endless Attempt

The previous chapter showed that natural selection is the judge. Now we must look at the courtroom itself — and at the defendant who is never acquitted there. For before there were people, before there were eyes and ears and fear, a process was already underway that no one had ordered and no one had designed. It is the simplest engine imaginable, and it has produced everything that lives.

There is no plan. There is a loop.

### The Loop Without a Designer

Nature does not work with a blueprint, not with a meeting, not with an intention. It works with variation and outcome. Something is attempted — a molecule that reacts differently, a protein that folds slightly differently, an offspring with a deviation — and then the outcome reveals whether it continues to exist or not. Then it begins again.

This is what it means that 'nature keeps trying': not that nature hopes, wills, or strives, but that it continues. It has no goal, no preference, and no hurry. It has only time, and a filter that knows no mercy.

What that filter says is precisely what you would expect from something without a mind: what has it easy enough to keep existing, keeps existing; what has it too hard to keep existing, does not keep existing. Not what is best, not what is most beautiful, not what is most just — only what proves tenable. Tenability is the only language the loop speaks.

### Four Steps, Four Billion Years

This is how life was built, layer upon layer, without anyone intending to make a living being.

Amino acids form spontaneously from the simplest molecules when conditions are favorable — the building blocks of proteins are not an invention of life; they existed before there was life. From those building blocks, chains arise that hold themselves stable. What falls apart, falls apart; what endures becomes the basis for the next round. Then come nucleotides, the letters of heredity. The exact route to them is still an open question in science, but the criterion does not change: a molecule that can copy itself multiplies; a molecule that cannot disappears without leaving a trace.

Then the cell. Fatty molecules fold spontaneously into little spheres in water — not a design, but an energetically easier state. When such a sphere also comes to contain content and begins to divide, something new exists: a bounded system that sustains itself. The cell arose because the chemistry of water could not do otherwise, not because anyone thought of it.

And then the leap that took almost two billion years to arrive: the eukaryote. One cell takes up another cell — an event that went wrong countless times, until one time it did not go wrong. The host did not perish, but was improved by it; the engulfed cell stayed alive as a power plant. From that accident come the mitochondria in every one of our cells, and the entire family of complex beings to which we belong.

Without a designer, without a promise. Nothing but attempts.

### Good Enough Is Enough

The strength of the loop lies in what it is not: it is not a genius, it is a sieve. It does not need to be clever, because it is allowed to be wrong without limit. What seems impossible in a single attempt becomes inevitable over billions of years and countless attempts. The loop needs no insight, no patience, and no morality — only time.

That explains both the beauty and the cruelty of nature. The same blindness that produced the eye also produced the parasite that drains its host. The same sieve that let our nervous system through also let suffering through. Nature does not choose; it sifts. Whoever praises nature for its creations must also acknowledge its victims: it is the same sieve, the same law, the same absence of a plan.

### The Price of Blindness

And there lies the limit. The loop works, but it works slowly, and it pays for every step with failures, with extinctions, with immeasurable pain endured. It cannot look ahead. It cannot distinguish between a solution that is good enough and a solution that would be good. It cannot tackle anything that requires planning.

That is why solutions are always left lying there that would work perfectly well: things the loop never finds by accident, because they require a series of deliberate steps. And that is why 'it is natural, therefore it is good' is the most dangerous fallacy there is. Nature did not make us with a plan, but it did leave us a method: try, see what endures, continue.

### The Loop Becomes Conscious

We now carry that method in consciousness. And that is the decisive transition in this book: the loop is no longer blind. Where nature took billions of years for a single improvement, we take a generation. The scientific method is in fact the same old loop — try, fail, adjust, repeat — but conscious, targeted, with memory, and without every failure having to cost a life. Selection moves from the outcome to the design.

In this way nature becomes our teacher instead of our fate. We take from it what works: the tireless willingness to keep trying, the honesty to acknowledge what does not work, and the hardness to let disappear what is not tenable. And we add what it never had: a purpose. The loop had no intention; we have one. We have become the attempt that understands it is an attempt — and that is precisely why it is now our task to choose what may continue to exist.
